# Tales of Dorlune: notes for Claude sessions

A Fairyland Online (2007) tribute RPG for iPhone: SwiftUI + SpriteKit, project generated by XcodeGen from
`project.yml`. Stay faithful to how Fairyland Online played and looked; check before inventing new systems.
Players know it as **Tales of Dorlune** (since 0.8.11, 2026-10-25: the App Store name and the logo; the Home
Screen shows **Tales of Dorlune** too (since 0.9.1; Dorlune before), project.yml `CFBundleDisplayName`, and the land itself, in every in-game line, is Dorlune;
it was Talewild from 0.8.1 to 0.8.10). The code and project are **TalesOfDorlune** (`TalesOfDorlune/`,
`TalesOfDorluneTests/`, the TalesOfDorlune target and scheme, types like `TalesOfDorluneSky`, the
`TALES_OF_DORLUNE_DEBUG` flags) and the repo tales-of-dorlune, since 2026-10-26; they were Storyleaf before, and
Fairyland before 2026-10-22. Never write Storyleaf anywhere new. Some things keep an old name on purpose, as
players' games are tied to them: the bundle ID (`com.jurgenploeger.fairyland`, which App Store Connect, TestFlight
and every player's saves are tied to), the save files' names (`fairyland-save.json`), and content ids saved in a
game (the weaver's `storyleaf_cape`, shown as Dorlune Cape). Don't bring back Fairyland Online's own names (towns,
people, bosses, quests) in player-facing text: `docs/fairyland-online.md` → Names.

## Layout
- `docs/fairyland-online.md`: how the original game (and Tales of Mysteria, its 2026 relaunch) worked, with
  sources, and where we differ. Read it before inventing a system; add facts you verify, with their source.
- `docs/continuous-world.md`: the plan for one continuous world (no borders, chunks streamed round you), in steps.
- `docs/sprite-acceptance.md`: what generated and fitted character art must pass (every body, direction and
  armour), how to check it, and what the Retro Diffusion wearables taught us. Read it before making or fitting any.
- `docs/pixel-style.md`: the game's pixel-art look, measured, for sprites drawn in code instead of by Retro
  Diffusion: the rules, the acceptance criteria (`tools/pixel_style.py check`, bands calibrated on every RD
  sprite the game ships, `tools/pixel_style.json`), the review by eye, and `tools/pixel_kit.py`, a renderer
  for the look (shapes and materials in, lit, textured, hue-shifted pixel art out). Read it before drawing
  a sprite in code; recalibrate after RD art is added.
- `docs/launch-audit.md`: the pre-launch audit (2026-10-06): App Store checklist, open bugs and balance issues
  ranked, and gamification ideas. Tick items off or remove them as they're done.
- Brand guidelines: the Figma file `521ECWenGbx2IP3rLgtIWo`, pages Cover to 09 Voice & Tone (logo, colour, type, icons,
  assets, game UI, App Store, voice). Its colours are variables (the Dorlune collection, with their Swift names; the text styles are Dorlune/…, the titles in Luckiest Guy); the logo is the component "Dorlune Logo" on the Workspace page, Luckiest Guy outlined to vectors;
  `Brand` in `TalesOfDorlune/UI/BrandSky.swift` holds the same values, so change both together.
- `content/world.json` + `tools/world_layout.py`: the lands as one continent, one landmass with one coast, each land a
  shape of its own (RuneScape-like regions, cold north, desert south). Edit world.json's `design` (each land's middle
  and lobes round it; a town's walled square, maps.json `town.size`, sits in its own countryside) and `rivers` (each
  one's course from its spring to the sea, or to a `lake` of its own) and rerun the tool (numpy + scipy): it
  paints the shapes and rivers, writes each land's box (maps.json `width`/`height`), middle, road crossings with
  their turns (a road into a cave ends inland, at the hill its mouth is in: `cave_gates`, `WorldMap.raiseGate`; a
  road by ship, maps.json exit `ship`, at a harbor on the coast, its pier out over the sea walked on though no land
  owns it: `harbors`, `World.pier`, `LandStage.placeHarbors`),
  and shape (`regions`, rivers in it), and `check_content.py` checks it (everything set down by hand on
  its land and clear of the rivers, roads meeting, nothing cut off by a river). Rerun it after changing exits or
  anything set down by hand; `--preview out.png` draws it. The game lays the rivers where they're painted, a bridge
  wherever a road crosses one (`WorldMap.layRivers`; of wood, stone, red lacquer or rope (its ropes sagging), `Bridge.Material`, drawn by `BridgeArt`, standing over the water: its thickness, piles, a shadow and a reflection along its near side; wood and rope break off raggedly on the banks; a road's stone or lacquer bridge over a river is a bow bridge, `Bridge.bowed`: a hump you walk over, `Bridge.bow`, `WorldMap.height(atGrid:)`, drawn as one picture by `BridgeArt.bowSprite`; `tools/bridge_preview.py` mirrors them; where the water's open on both sides of it if anywhere near) along a row or column, so the tool runs each river straight along the other
  where a road crosses it (`SQUARE_REACH`; aslant, its bridge crossed it askew); the land slopes down to the sea and into the lakes
  (`WorldMap.shore`, drawn sunk by `HillArt`, darker the lower it lies; the sea paler in the shallows, `ContinentScene.shallows`:
  a distance field, after SpriteCook's shoreline article, each chunk's distances from the land worked out once,
  `shallowField`, 4 spots a cell, and drawn by `shallowShader` on the sea's own pixels, bands that round every bay and
  point and broken crests rolling in; `tools/shore_art.py` mirrors it, before and after or `--gif`). `TalesOfDorlune/World/Region.swift` is a land's shape; `Continent.swift` reads
  them: neighbours where shapes touch, open and walled borders (a border opens along its length once a road you can
  take joins the two lands, so none skips a quest), and the sea all round, each cell drawn by the nearest land
  (`BorderArt` draws the fences, rocks and city walls along the cell edges). Boxes overlap: a land lays out and
  draws only its own cells (`WorldMap.owns`; the rest is `.sea` or `.away`). `Seam.swift` is the band where one
  land's ground and scenery give way to the next's in patches, measured from the border (14 cells each side, fewer where
  either land's maps.json theme `blend` says: Briarmere's and Snow White Forest's snow, beside rock, 5); its `Blend` says what
  any cell shows the same from every land, so a tile meets the one past the border (that land's) cleanly.
  The world map (`WorldAtlas`) shows every land's minimap where it lies (`WorldOverview`, cached on disk).
  The continent is one scene (`ContinentScene`, docs/continuous-world.md): `World` holds which land owns each world
  cell and where you can walk; each land near you is laid out, planned (`LandPlanner`) and drawn a 32-cell chunk at a
  time round you, its ground by `GroundTiles`, its hills and shore by `HillArt.painter`, what it sets down and its
  people by `LandStage`, in its own points at its shift. Lands are laid out one at a time, nearest first (the one
  you're in before any), and drawn in their plain ground till then, walked on as their shape has it (anywhere but
  their rivers and lakes, `World.walkable`); the world map's own layouts wait meanwhile
  (`WorldOverview.isHeld`). A land over 176 cells off is let go (`letGoFarLands`: memory grew past a gigabyte) and built again, the same, when you're back. You walk anywhere with no loading card; the land you're in
  (`here`) brings its music, air and encounters. Caves are scenes of their own (`WorldScene`), behind the card;
  `GameCoordinator.mapScene` is whichever you're in.
- Terraced hills (maps.json theme `hills.terraces`, a step in points, with `bank` the faces' rock; Sunny Meadow
  since 0.9.105, after `tools/cliff_preview.py`, every wild land's since 0.9.110; a mountain too since 0.9.189, in five levels, `WorldMap.mountainLevels`, its face
  nearly as deep as its back, stairs cut across each face as ledges zig-zagging up it (not drawn as a path: messy, the user), `carveStairs`,
  the user's pick from `tools/mountain_preview.py`, 2026-11-25): `Hill.terrace` steps each range's height into flat levels with a
  sheer face, only where its slopes face you (`Hill.stepped`: stepped all round, the faces turned away were laid
  back by `unfold()` into long ramps in deep shade), the faces dwindling round its sides and its back the smooth
  slope you walk up (no trails);
  `WorldMap.blockFaces` blocks the faces and keeps the cell in front clear; `HillLooks.terraced` (`HillArt.Look.cliff`): the
  faces' rock is drawn by the lifting shader on the screen's pixels (`u_rock`, by height and across the screen:
  painted into the picture it stretched into streaks), with shade at each face's foot; up to two waterfalls a
  range over faces turned to you (`LandPlanner.planCliffFalls`, `LandStage.placeCliffFalls`: a spring pond on the level above, a stream, a sheet
  stretched to the face, a pond at its foot, from `tools/waterfall_art.py`; drawn, not the land's own water).
- A town's terraces (maps.json town `terraces`, `WorldMap.raiseTerrace`, 34 points high) in stacked sandstone
  (the user's pick from `tools/terrace_preview.py`, 2026-11-17): `tools/terrace_art.py` draws a cell's stretch of
  wall (sheared along its edge), of balustrade, a post and the flagstones; `LandStage.placePlateaus` lays them,
  `placeTerraces` the balustrade round the top, and the terrace casts its shade away from the sun as a block
  (`CanopyShade.addBlock`). Its top is sorted behind its back corner, so whoever stands on the ground behind it
  is sorted behind that (`WorldMap.depth(at:)`, `depthBehindTerrace`; they showed through it, the user, 2026-11-18). A cobbled path runs from the foot of each stair to the nearest street (`streetLink`), and nothing
  is built in the ring round it or two cells behind (the user, 2026-11-18). Caves' `WorldScene` keeps the old look.
- How a blow lands in battle (`BattleScene.impact`, after SpriteCook's hit feel article): attacker and
  target stand still 5 frames (9 on a killing blow, `hitStop`: the two nodes paused, the stage plays
  on), the target white for 2 (`whiteFlash`, a shader: a tint can't light a dark outline), then pushed
  away and back in whole points (`knockback`) as its red tint plays; a critical or a killing blow
  shakes the field in whole points (`shake`, not with Reduce Motion). docs/pixel-style.md *Effects on
  the pixel grid*.
- A cave's light stops at its rock (`WorldScene.castLight`, after SpriteCook's shadowcasting
  article): `Shadowcast.visible` (recursive shadowcasting on the cave's cells, recast as you step into
  another cell) says which cells round you the light sees; `Lantern.shade` lays soft dark over the rest
  inside its circle, and the cave's map fills in only with what it saw. `tools/shadowcast.py` mirrors
  it with the article's tests; keep the two in step. What it sees also catches the light on its side
  toward you (`LanternLight`: the street lamps' outline normals, `LampLight.copy`, re-aimed from where
  you stand each step; cave scenery, its lit copies made the first time the light reaches it. Not the
  rock's blocks: lit one by one, every seam between them glowed and their light fell on whoever stood
  behind them).
- Shade under the trees (`CanopyShade`, the user's pick from `tools/canopy_preview.py`, 2026-11-14): on the
  continent everything `LandStage.addScenery` sets down on flat ground (trees, buildings, lamps, rocks; not
  under 12 points) lays its own outline on the ground away from the sun, each chunk's pixels worked out off
  the main thread (`CanopyPainter.render`: on it, the game went slow), a round
  slice per row of it (a building's set back from its front by its depth, a block: as thin bands its midday shade was a sliver, 2026-11-18;
  a gate's arch thin bands, `Caster.thin`; a rock or boulder a block too, `CanopyShade.isRock`), the top of the crown farthest (short at midday, long at evening), all merged into one,
  deepest at the foot and fading toward the tip, its edge blurred (`softness`; in hard steps it was too stark), soft flecks of light where the leaves part, and multiplied over the ground
  as the night's shade is, by one cool tint in every land (`CanopyShade.lean`: each palette's own shadow
  colour turned a desert red), stopping at a town's wall (`blocks`, `WorldMap.fenceCells`), which casts its own, as do its towers and gates (`LandStage.placeFence`, `throughWalls`); drawn a `LandStage` chunk at a time while shown, again as the sun moves (`follow`,
  every half second from `ContinentScene.updateAir`, which casts walkers' shadows too: `Walker.shine`). A
  walker's shadow is its own frame laid the same way, as long for its height (`Walker.figure`, a dark copy
  of the sprite's texture each frame, softened and fading toward its head by `Walker.soften`, kept just over the ground, `figureDepth`), with a soft round at its feet; both stand on the lowest row its first frame draws (`Walker.feet`, `standFigure`: hung off the sprite's anchor, a few pixels under the feet, they read as hovering, the user, 2026-11-18). On a
  hill or the sunk shore, and in caves, a prop keeps its round `Lighting.Shadow`. Keep the tool and the Swift in step.

  shading; a bare `shift` lands on a different colour for every base, and a window that cuts a ramp splits it in two.
  Never swap a hero's whole sheet for a townsperson: hair shares its shades with skin and boots, so the deep reds and
  pale tips stay ginger. Derive them with `layers` instead (body, locks, hair from `tools/hero_layers.py`, the hair's
  rules widened to appearance.json `hairLayer`), as every quest giver is; `check_content.py` insists.

  <ids> --new` and `review` beside its neighbours; Retro Diffusion's styles, prices and prompts in its `references/`).
- `art/eyes.json`: where every sprite's eyes are, so it blinks (`EyeBlink`, `TalesOfDorlune/Art/IdlePoses.swift`):
  a single picture's (a lash drawn across each shut eye), a walk sheet's in each direction's standing pose (shut
  under its own lid); a body layer's stand for its head layer, a copy shuts its base's. The eyes are shut on the
  PNG or layer before it's recoloured and dressed (`ArtLibrary.idlePoses`), so hair and masks drawn over them
  still are. Never a wink: both eyes shut or neither (`EyeBlink.shutsEvenly`), and no blink where a helmet's or
  an armour sheet's own face shows round the eyes (`ArtLibrary.cleanFaces`). Standing still, a walk sheet also breathes in whole
  pixels (`EyeBlink.exhaled`: everything above the waist a pixel lower). A new monster or body needs its eyes

  and the festive seasons' hats, masks and scarves and the status capes go on from pieces `tools/wear_art.py` cut out.
- `tools/worn_marks.py`: where gloves and boots go on each hero body, marked once, frame by frame
  (`art/sprites/marks_<body>.png`: red a glove, blue a boot, the paler of each its band), which
  `GearOverlay.trinkets` and `tools/gear_preview.py` paint; found as skin while dressing, a body with white
  cuffs took no gloves and a neck took them as a bib. Rerun it after `hero_layers.py` changes a body;
  `--sheet out.png` shows the marks on every frame.
- `tools/armour_fit.py`: armour sheets fitted to the bodies a race's own sheet doesn't reach. A race's sheet
  is worn by its three bodies under each one's own head; where a body's head ends two rows or more above the
  collar in a frame, that body gets every sheet of its race again (`<sheet>_fit_<body>.png`, and `_trim`), the
  armour a little taller in that frame: rows between collar and hands drawn twice, so the shoulders meet the
  head and feet, legs and hands stay put. classes.json lists the bodies (a race's `fitted`), which
  `GameSession.layers`, `gear_preview.py` and `eyes.py` read. Never move a frame up (the feet leave the
  ground) or raise only the collar (a dark block under the chin). Rerun after `wear_art.py`, `hero_layers.py`
  or `dye_art.py cut`; `--report` lists the bare neck rows, `--sheet out.png` shows before and after.
- `tools/wear_art.py`: worn gear from Retro Diffusion sheets in `art-variants/` (picked by hand, its `ARMOUR` and
  `WEAR`; sheets older than these notes in `art-variants/legacy/`), fixed in code: every armour walk sheet
  (`armor_<item>_<race>`, items.json `sheets`) with its main material brought to one colour on every race
  (`MATERIAL`), so it and each tier's `tint` look alike on all; and the pieces cut out of a hero wearing them
  (`wear_<hat>`, `wear_cape`), moved to fixed anchors. The game fits them to each frame as drawn (GearOverlay; mirror in
  `tools/gear_preview.py`, `--wear out.png <ids>` shows items on every body under every armour): a hat widened to the
  head, its band on the head's widest row over the eyes, the hair above it cleared; a mask on the eyes as drawn, no
  taller than the face, from the side as far forward as it reaches; a scarf under the chin as drawn; a cape hung from
  the shoulders, stretched to the body. Rerun after picking a sheet; `docs/sprite-acceptance.md` says what to check.
- `tools/rd.py`: Retro Diffusion client. `rd.py edit` inpaints a mask of an existing sheet (pilot 2026-10-19: inpainting
  of walk sheets is a dead end: faces, about 10 px wide, come out muddy, and armour or a robe painted onto a hero comes
  out too bulky, with side and back views that don't match the pose), its results only in
  `art-variants/<name>/` (`rd.py fetch` gets any that didn't download). `tools/character_art.py` makes the masks (face,
  body, helmet) from `hero_layers.py`'s heads, into `art-variants/_masks/`, outside `art/` so they never ship. `tools/icons.py`: vendors Iconaut icons (MIT), plus our own drawings in
  `art/icons/` where Iconaut has no shape that works (fire's three-tongued flame).
  `tools/check_content.py`: validates the game data.
- `tools/wang_art.py`: the land's plain ground as edge Wang tiles (`ArtLibrary.wangTile`,
  `GroundTiles.wang`, after SpriteCook's Wang tiles article): 16 versions of each ground tile, each edge
  the tile's own strip or a shifted one, hashed from where it lies in the world, and the tile's own
  strip wherever the cell across isn't plain ground of the same tile (roads, ponds, patches, shores and
  the land next door meet the original, as before). Keep it, the Swift and `palette_preview.py` in
  step; `check` holds every edge of a field to the tile's own seams, `--preview out.png` shows before
  and after, near and zoomed out.
- `tools/blind_review.py`: is code's art as good as Retro Diffusion's? Pairs of one drawn in code and
  one of RD's of the same kind, sides shuffled, the key in a file of its own the user doesn't see
  till they've picked; `score` tallies. The goal and its stages: the sprite-creation skill, *Drawing as
  well as Retro Diffusion*.
- `tools/pixel_scale.py`: how big each sprite's art pixels are on the map, in points (its PNG times its
  `scale`; a ground tile 44 points over 32 pixels), against a walker's 1, and the props planted at a
  random size (maps.json theme props' `size`); `--sheet out.png` lines them up at game size. Its rule:
  docs/sprite-acceptance.md *One art pixel, one size*. Flowers are planted at 0.55 to 0.7, their pixels
  smaller than their neighbours' by the user's choice (2026-11-19: "a flower shouldn't be just as tall as a tree").
- `tools/deslab.py`: cuts the raised slab of ground some generated buildings came with (`inn`, `magic_tower`) out
  of their sprites, so they stand on the map's ground; rerun it after regenerating one of them.
- `tools/rd_fixes.py`: exact pixel fixes to RD sprites for what RD got wrong and the user asked to have fixed
  (the Poison Skeleton's stray green spark, which every recolour of it inherited): each lists the pixels and
  the colours they must have first, so a rerun changes nothing and a regenerated sprite is reported, not spoiled.
- `theme.palette` in `content/maps.json` colour-grades each map's ground, scenery and buildings at load time.
  `tools/palette_preview.py` (needs pillow + numpy) renders every map with and without it, to tune without a Mac.
- `content/i18n/`: translations. `languages.json` lists the languages (the picker's order), `<code>.json` maps each
  English string to its translation, `fields.json` names the content keys that hold text. Swift text goes through
  `L("English {placeholder}", ["placeholder": value])` (`TalesOfDorlune/Model/Localization.swift`); content text is
  translated as it loads (`Content.reload` after a switch). `tools/i18n.py status | missing <code> | merge | prune`.
- `content/music.json` defines instruments and songs for `TalesOfDorlune/Audio/SongSynth.swift`. `tools/music_preview.py`
  (needs numpy + scipy) renders songs to WAV with the same algorithm; keep the two in step.
- `sound/*.wav`: sound effects, synthesized by `tools/make_sounds.py` (needs numpy + scipy; 44.1 kHz stereo,
  byte-identical on every rerun). Edit a recipe and rerun (`--report` prints each file's length and loudness);
  `SoundEffects.Sound` in `TalesOfDorlune/Audio/SoundEffects.swift` lists them. Settings live in `GameSettings` (UserDefaults).

## Rules
- **Cloud sessions (Linux) can't build or run the app.** After editing `content/` or `art/assets.json`, run
  `python3 tools/check_content.py`. Keep Swift edits small and careful: nothing compiles a `claude/**`
  branch by itself (a Screenshots run by hand does, below), only `.github/workflows/testflight.yml` after a push to `main` (on GitHub's `xcode-27` runner, a release Xcode 27 and the iOS 27 SDK
  App Store Connect asks for from April 2027; the Screenshots run can pick it too, input `runner`), so a mistake fails that
  TestFlight build (Xcode Cloud isn't used: its GitHub app was uninstalled on 2026-10-23). Read every
  changed line for what the compiler would catch (a name renamed in one place and not another broke 0.9.42).
- **Never redraw Retro Diffusion's art in code** (the user, 2026-10-24: "that's actually really good"). Every
  asset with a `request` in art/assets.json is RD's and stays as it is, and so does what's `derive`d from it.
  Draw in code only what RD never made, or what it got wrong and the user asked to have fixed.
- **Retro Diffusion costs money.** Always run `python3 tools/rd.py generate <ids> --dry-run` (free) and get
  the user's OK before generating. Paid runs are logged in `art/spend-log.jsonl`. Never commit `.env` or the
  API key (`RETRO_DIFFUSION_API_KEY`).
- **Every skill looks different** (a playtest rule): each skill used in battle has its own `animation` in
  `content/skills.json`, drawn by `TalesOfDorlune/Battle/SkillStyles.swift` (or `ElementEffects.swift` for the elemental
  spells) and dispatched in `BattleScene.castSkill`. `check_content.py` rejects a shared or unknown one, so a new skill
  needs a new effect, in light and its own colours.
- **Every player-visible string is translated:** wrap new Swift text in `L("…")` (a single-line literal; values
  as `{placeholders}`, never `\(` inside the key), then fill in all ten languages with `tools/i18n.py missing <code>`
  and `merge`. Text in content fields listed in `content/i18n/fields.json` needs the same. Untranslated text shows in
  English; `check_content.py` rejects lost placeholders.
- **Versions:** `MARKETING_VERSION` in `project.yml` and the top entry of `content/changelog.json` move together
  (the checker enforces it). What's new sums up big updates, not every patch: one entry per update with at most
  five short lines (the checker enforces it). A small user-visible change moves the top entry's `version` and
  `date` along and folds into its lines; start a new entry only for a big update. 1.0.0 = App Store launch.
- **Screenshots and tests:** `.github/workflows/screenshots.yml` runs **by hand only** (`workflow_dispatch`,
  on any branch: the GitHub MCP's `actions_run_trigger` with `run_workflow`, workflow `screenshots.yml`, the
  branch as `ref` and inputs `run` (tests, screenshots or both) and `scenes`, comma-separated names from
  `tools/screenshots.txt`). It builds for the simulator, runs the unit tests and shoots the scenes it names
  (TALES_OF_DORLUNE_DEBUG flags), publishing them to the `screenshots` branch under `<branch>/`: fetch with
  `git fetch --depth=1 --filter=blob:none origin screenshots`, then `git show origin/screenshots:<branch>/<scene>.png`.
  Name the few scenes you need: all of them take two and a half hours. It ran on every push until the user
  removed it (2026-11-01: it "keeps eating all our minutes", the repository was private); back by hand
  since 2026-11-10, the repository public and macOS runners free. Never make it, or any workflow, run on
  pushes without the user's say. On a Mac, `xcodebuild test` and `tools/screenshots.sh` do the same.
  Say plainly what hasn't been tested or seen.
- On a Mac: `make open` or `make sim`; tests with `xcodebuild test` (see README). Tests and debug launches use
  separate save files, so they never touch the player's save.
